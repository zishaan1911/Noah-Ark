# Ark-1 flight: pontoon trigger

ArduPilot Lua script that inflates Ark-1's two CO2 pontoons for a water landing, plus the parameters it needs.
The hardware and all numbers come from `../industrial-design/pontoon_system.py` (output: `pontoon_system.json`).

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `scripts/pontoon_trigger.lua` | The trigger. Copy it to `APM/scripts/` on the Pixhawk 6X SD card (Copter 4.7+; tested in SITL, see ../../simulation). |
| `pontoon.parm` | Params, generated by `pontoon_system.py`. Load in Mission Planner / QGC, then reboot. |

Test (from `hardware/`): `python -m pytest -q industrial-design/tests/test_pontoon_trigger.py`.
It runs the real script in Lua 5.3 (`pip install lupa`) against mocked ArduPilot bindings and a simulated descent.
Without `lupa` the test is skipped.

## The hardware, per side

```
 harness ── 4-pin connector (fixed point: x 0.24, y ±0.251, z -0.535 m)
              │  inflator_L.fire (2 wires)        pontoon_L.pressure_ok (2 wires)
              ▼                                    ▲
   latch solenoid releases a hand-cocked spring ─► pulls the lever of a Halkey-Roberts manual inflator head
                                                   │ pin pierces the 60 g CO2 cylinder (1/2-20 UNF)
                                                   ▼
                     outlet ─ tee ─ pressure switch (PSF102, closes at 12 kPa)
                                └─ hose ─ inlet check valve ─ bladder (15.3 L, 210D TPU-nylon)
                                                              └─ relief valve (35 kPa)
```

- No pyrotechnics: the spring does the work, the solenoid only releases it (12 V, 0.5 A, 300 ms, about 1.8 J).
  Re-arm = screw in a new cylinder, re-cock the spring, repack the bladder, set `PONT_FIRED = 0`.
- Firing needs two outputs: **ARM** (high-side switch feeding both solenoids, relay 1) and **FIRE_L / FIRE_R**
  (low-side, relays 2 and 3). One stuck output cannot fire a side. A remove-before-flight pin locks each spring
  on the ground.
- Pressure switch closes to GND; the FMU pin has a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V. A broken wire reads "no pressure",
  never a false OK.

| Pixhawk 6X pin | Function | Param |
|---|---|---|
| AUX3 (GPIO 52) | ARM | `RELAY1_PIN` |
| AUX4 (GPIO 53) | FIRE_L | `RELAY2_PIN` |
| AUX5 (GPIO 54) | FIRE_R | `RELAY3_PIN` |
| AUX6 (GPIO 55) | pressure switch L | `PONT_PS_L_PIN` |
| AUX7 (GPIO 56) | pressure switch R | `PONT_PS_R_PIN` |

## The sequence

1. **Arm.** The script arms when the vehicle is armed and flying and either
   - **planned water landing:** `PONT_PLAN_WTR = 1` (the Ark Brain mission planner sets it before uploading a
     water-landing mission) and the vehicle is landing (LAND mode, or AUTO on a `NAV_LAND` item), or
   - **emergency ditching:** battery failsafe, and the companion's water classifier says water
     (`PONT_OVERWTR = 1`) or its flag is stale (`PONT_WTR_SEQ` unchanged for 5 s). Unknown counts as water:
     a wasted cylinder costs less than the drone. Over known land it stays disarmed.
2. **Fire** both sides when the skid height above the water (radar minus `PONT_RNG_OFS`) drops to
   `max(PONT_FIRE_ALT, descent rate × PONT_INF_T × PONT_MARGIN + PONT_ABORT_ALT)`.
   With the default 0.5 m/s landing descent that is **7.0 m**: inflation needs 7.2 s in the cold case
   (15 °C, 3.7 s at 30 °C), fired 14 s before touchdown. A faster descent fires higher (1.5 m/s: 17.7 m,
   which is what LAND does above 10 m; the SITL water landing fired at 17.2 m).
   `PONT_FIRED` is latched and saved *before* the pulse, so the script can never fire twice, even after a reboot.
3. **Confirm.** Each pressure switch should close by about 3.7 s (30 °C) to 7.1 s (15 °C) after FIRE.
4. **Retry.** At `PONT_RETRY_T` (7 s) a side that has no pressure gets one more pulse (a stuck latch leaves the
   cylinder full). On a planned landing the drone also stops descending (GUIDED hold: LOITER would read the missing pilot throttle as full
   down, which the SITL run showed) while it waits.
5. **Verdict** at `max(PONT_OK_TMO, PONT_RETRY_T + PONT_INF_T)`:
   - both OK: landing continues (or resumes from the hold),
   - a side failed on a **planned** landing: the drone holds in GUIDED above the abort height and the GCS gets
     an EMERGENCY message; the operator or the Ark Brain diverts it to land,
   - a side failed in an **emergency**: it lands anyway (no battery to go elsewhere), with a warning to expect a list.

Why the abort: one tube alone still floats the drone (1.005 × its weight) but it lists about 49° onto that side
and the low props go into the water. Recoverable, not flyable.

Pre-arm checks (ArduPilot aux auth): take-off is blocked while `PONT_FIRED = 1` with soft tubes (kit spent), or
while a pressure switch reads closed before firing (bag out, or a shorted lead). A fired kit with both tubes still
holding pressure is the drone floating after a water landing, so it may take off from the water again.

Every 50 ms the script logs `PONT` (state, height, descent rate, L/R pressure) to the dataflash log, and it sends
each event to the GCS as `PONT: ...` text.

## Parameters

| Param | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `PONT_ENABLE` | 1 | master enable |
| `PONT_PLAN_WTR` | 0 | 1 = this mission ends in a planned water landing |
| `PONT_OVERWTR`, `PONT_WTR_SEQ` | 0 | companion water flag + a counter it bumps on every update |
| `PONT_FIRE_ALT` | 7.0 m | minimum firing height (skid to water) |
| `PONT_INF_T` | 7.2 s | FIRE to pressure OK, cold case |
| `PONT_MARGIN` | 1.5 | timing margin on the inflation time |
| `PONT_ABORT_ALT` | 1.5 m | lowest height at which a planned landing is still aborted |
| `PONT_OK_TMO` | 14.5 s | give up waiting for pressure |
| `PONT_RETRY_T` | 8.2 s | one re-pulse for a side with no pressure |
| `PONT_RNG_OFS` | 0.34 m | radar face to skid bottom |
| `PONT_PULSE_MS` | 300 | solenoid pulse |
| `PONT_FIRED` | 0 | latch; ground crew clears it after re-arming the kit |
| `PONT_PS_L_PIN`, `PONT_PS_R_PIN` | 55, 56 | pressure-switch GPIOs |
| `PONT_RLY_ARM`, `PONT_RLY_L`, `PONT_RLY_R` | 0, 1, 2 | relay instances (0 = RELAY1) |

## Estimates to replace on the bench

The inflation time, the gas left as dry ice, and the actuator force are model estimates (marked "est." in
`pontoon_system.json`). Before trusting `PONT_INF_T`:

1. Fire one kit 10 times at 15 °C and 30 °C, with the cylinder neck down as mounted; log the bag pressure
   (1 kHz transducer on the tee) and the switch closing time. Set `PONT_INF_T` to the worst case.
2. Check the head does not ice up: neck-down mounting draws liquid CO2, which is faster but colder than the
   vapour-only model.
3. Measure the lever pull force of the inflator head and the spring's force at the end of its stroke (target 2 ×).
4. Leave an inflated bag in the sun for an hour: the relief valve should hold it at 35 kPa.

## Limits

- `shortcut:` the companion's water flag travels as a parameter write (PARAM_SET), not a MAVLink message; upgrade
  to a `mavlink:register_rx_msgid` NAMED_VALUE_INT reader if param writes prove too slow or too chatty.
- `shortcut:` no solenoid continuity check before flight; add a 10 kΩ sense resistor and an ADC read when the
  driver board is laid out.
- The radar is the only height source over water; if it drops out the script falls back to `ahrs:get_hagl()`
  (terrain database), which over a flood plain may be metres off.
